01 / ABOUT PARON

Offline doesn’t have to mean
payment stops.

Paron is designed for moments where connectivity is spotty or unavailable: metro stations, flights, basements, and rural outposts. Before stepping offline, the customer reserves an allowance backed by their PayPal account.

A nearby merchant can immediately accept an asymmetric cryptographic receipt. Once either side reconnects, the backend reconciliation engine verifies signatures and settles the transaction against the ledger.

04 / HACKATHON FAQ

Everything you need to know.

Straightforward answers on our security model, PayPal integration, and edge cases.

Paron uses a prepaid allowance model. When online, the user locks a specific amount (e.g. ₹500) from their PayPal balance into a one-time reservation token. Even if a user copies their offline token to another device, the Sync Service enforces atomic cumulative spend checks on reconciliation: the first valid receipt settles, and conflicting claims exceeding the cap are rejected. Total financial exposure is strictly bounded to the prepaid allowance.
In an offline system, no server or AI model can physically stop a dishonest actor from handing over a signed receipt for a cup of coffee offline. Claiming "zero-risk offline payments" is mathematically dishonest. Instead, Paron limits risk to a prepaid allowance, cryptographically verifies authenticity via ECDSA P-256, and uses post-reconnection AI reasoning to adjudicate disputes and flag suspicious merchants or velocity patterns.
Paron is presented as a "PayPal-funded offline wallet with safe reconciliation." PayPal is the authoritative funding source: users top up their wallet using PayPal Sandbox Orders and Captures. Reservations lock funds against that balance, and the ledger settles merchant claims back to the system upon reconnection.
