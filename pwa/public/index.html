ABOUT PARON

Offline doesn’t have to mean
payment stops.

Paron is for places where internet access may be unreliable, such as transit stations or rural areas. While online, a customer sets aside part of their wallet balance as an offline spending limit. This website uses example values only; the Android app demonstrates the connected funding flow.

When a customer pays offline, the merchant keeps a payment receipt. After the devices reconnect, Paron’s online service checks the receipt and spending limit before updating the merchant’s account record. This website simulates the nearby handoff; it does not connect to a real merchant phone.

COMMON FAQ

Everything you need to know.

Plain answers about offline limits, payment receipts, and the browser simulation.

Before losing internet access, the customer sets aside an amount for offline purchases. When receipts later reach Paron’s online service, it compares the total with that limit and looks for repeated payments. A merchant may already have handed over goods before those checks happen, so offline payments still carry risk. The website walkthrough uses sample data; it does not reserve or move real money.
While devices have no internet, a server cannot check a payment as it happens. A merchant might accept a receipt that is later rejected during syncing. Paron limits the amount available for offline spending and checks receipts after reconnecting; this reduces risk but cannot remove it completely.
No. This website is a local simulation: its sample balance, offline limit, receipts, and sync result are stored only in your browser. It does not connect to PayPal or Paron backend services. PayPal Sandbox funding is part of the Android app/backend flow.
The project includes a native Android app for demonstrating the phone experience. This website is a separate judge walkthrough: its phone-to-phone payment animation is simulated and does not use Bluetooth or Android secure hardware. Use the Android app to show the device flow.
